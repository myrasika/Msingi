Rights of Arrested Persons

Article 49 • Chapter Four: The Bill of Rights

lightbulb

What this means in plain language

Your inviolable protections during custody or detention

1

Prompt reason for arrest

You must be informed immediately, in a language you fully understand, why you are being placed under arrest.

2

Absolute right to remain silent

You do not have to answer questions or make any statement. Choosing silence can never be used against you in court as evidence of guilt.

3

Private legal counsel & contact

You are entitled to communicate promptly with an advocate of your choice, and a doctor, spouse, or next of kin in complete privacy.

4

24-hour court appearance

Police officers cannot hold you arbitrarily. You must be brought before a magistrate or judge within 24 hours of arrest.

warning
Important Timing Exception

If the 24-hour period expires on a weekend or public holiday, you must be brought before a court of law not later than noon of the next court day.

Constitution of Kenya, 2010

National Council for Law Reporting (Kenya Law)
Statute
ARTICLE 49(1)(a)–(h) — RIGHTS OF ARRESTED PERSONS

"(1) An arrested person has the right—"

(a) to be informed promptly, in language that the person understands, of the reason for the arrest, the right to remain silent, and the consequences of not remaining silent;

(b) to remain silent;

(c) to communicate with an advocate, and other persons whose assistance is necessary;

(d) not to be compelled to make any confession or admission that could be used in evidence against the person;

(e) to be held separately from persons who are serving a sentence;

(f) to be brought before a court as soon as reasonably possible, but not later than twenty-four hours after being arrested; or if the twenty-four hours ends outside ordinary court hours, or on a day that is not an ordinary court day, the end of the next court day;

(g) at the first court appearance, to be charged or informed of the reason for the detention continuing, or to be released; and

(h) to be released on bond or bail, on reasonable conditions, pending a charge or trial, unless there are compelling reasons not to be released."
