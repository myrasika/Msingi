You have rights

The Constitution protects you, even when you are arrested. Here's what you need to know and do.

verified Constitution of Kenya 2010
1

Ask why you are being arrested

The police must tell you the reason for your arrest. You have the right to know promptly and in a language you understand.

2

Remember your right to remain silent

You are not required to answer questions that may incriminate you or make any confession that could be used against you in court.

3

Ask to contact an advocate

You have the right to communicate with an advocate or lawyer, and to consult with them privately without undue delay.

4

Understand the court time limit

You must be brought before a court within 24 hours (or as soon as reasonably possible after the expiry of 24 hours) as required by the Constitution.

Article 49(1)(a) & (b)

“An arrested person has the right to be informed promptly, in language that the person understands, of the reason for the arrest, the right to remain silent, and the consequences of not remaining silent...”

Article 49(1)(f)

“...to be brought before a court as soon as reasonably possible, but not later than twenty-four hours after being arrested; or if the twenty-four hours ends outside ordinary court hours, before the end of the next court day.”
